## 3. The Escalation Limiter — and a second one nobody has named

**The kinetic limiter is unchanged and still American.** Every regional actor has struck energy infrastructure — Iran at Ras Laffan and Kuwait, Israel at Asaluyeh, the UAE at Lavan, the Houthis at Jazan twice, Ukraine across Russia weekly. **Only the United States has not struck Iranian energy infrastructure.** The prepared campaign against power plants and petrochemical facilities still awaits Trump's authorisation — **now eighteen days un-given.**

**But today reveals a second limiter, and it runs the other way.** A fee-and-permission regime is a **business**. At $1–2m a vessel across the traffic still moving, Iran now has a **revenue stream that only exists while ships transit**. Closing the strait completely would zero it. **Iran has acquired a financial interest in the strait staying partly open** — which is a more reliable brake than restraint, and it is consistent with capacity sitting at 45% rather than near zero.

**What breaks each:** the American limiter breaks on one signature. **The Iranian one breaks if the toll stops being paid** — if enough owners refuse and route around, the revenue argument for keeping the lane open disappears and only the blockade logic remains.

## 4. The Economic & Sanctions Front — T-2, and the toll is now part of the cost stack

**The OFAC general license expires 12:01 AM EDT Friday, August 21 — two days.** No renewal reported. The instrument was bolted to the 60-day MOU, **which expired Monday with no extension**, so the affirmative act required to renew has lost its stated rationale.

What lapses together: unlimited sales; **dollar payment for oil, freight *and* insurance**; **use of previously sanctioned vessels**; **proceeds direct to Iran's central bank**. Points 2–4 are plumbing, and plumbing takes longer to rebuild than a trade flow.

**Who buys: China, ~90% of Iranian exports**, teapots the bulk. Iranian discounts widened from ~$0.50–1.00 under Brent in early 2025 to **$8–10 under** by year-end; Russia and Iran are cutting against each other for the same shrinking Chinese pool as India retreats under US tariff pressure. **Sanctioned barrels are not leaving the market, they are being repriced into it.**

**Enforcement credibility: targeted, not comprehensive.** Washington eased restrictions specifically to avoid a supply shock for Asian buyers — an admission full enforcement is too expensive. The Senate's 100%-tariff bill remains **unenacted**. But State Department action against shadow-fleet operators continued through July 29 *while the waiver was live*.

## 5. OPEC+ — the hiking cycle ends in September

**August 2 decision:** **Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, Oman** agreed **+188 kb/d** for September from the April 2023 additional voluntary adjustments. **OPEC+ will halt further increases after September** — the hiking cycle that began post-closure ends there.

> **Fourth day flagged: I have not obtained the per-country kb/d split and am not inventing one.** It needs pulling from the OPEC secretariat release directly rather than from secondary coverage. This is now a standing gap, not a one-off omission.

**The quota-versus-delivery gap is the story.** Quota-participating production averaged **27,468 kb/d in July** — highest since before the war — while delegates concede major members cannot deliver assigned hikes. Analysts estimate actual Gulf output is down **several hundred thousand b/d** on damaged infrastructure, diverted shipping and precautionary shutdowns.

**Spare capacity is near zero**, Saudi holding the only meaningful buffer — and **Saudi barrels reach water only through Hormuz (45%, now tolled) or Yanbu into Bab el-Mandeb (62%).**

*Caveat carried: several secondary sources frame this as "post-UAE exit." I have not confirmed a UAE departure from OPEC+ and am not treating it as established.*

## 6. Second-Order Politics

- **The two proposals on the table are now specific and incompatible.** Oman has put a **Gulf-backed plan for JOINT regional management with VOLUNTARY fees** to Iran. Iran wants **unilateral control with mandatory tolls** and is already collecting. **The gap is not the money** — $1–2m a vessel is small against a $10–14m war-risk premium — **it is sovereignty over an international waterway.** That is why five weeks of "close to a deal" produced nothing: the parties are deadlocked on a binary, not converging on a number.
- **Oman remains under a US bombing threat** while hosting the only live framework. If Muscat withdraws there is no channel.
- **A toll regime creates precedent risk far beyond Hormuz.** If a littoral state can price transit through an international strait and enforce it by boarding, **Bab el-Mandeb, Malacca and the Bosphorus all become negotiable.** That is the second-order risk nobody is pricing.
- **EIA: Middle East production not back near pre-conflict levels until early 2027**, Brent averaging $87 for 2026.
- **Against it:** the IEA cut 2026 demand 1.6 mb/d on price-driven destruction while doubling the Q3 deficit to 1.8 mb/d. **A deficit met by demand destruction caps upside even as it justifies the floor.**
- **Interceptor-stock depletion** across the Gulf allies remains the unmodelled tail risk.

## 9. Bypass Infrastructure — the clock on the bull thesis

| Saudi expansion | +2–4 mb/d | **0** | **no FID**; pre-2030 if taken promptly |
| **Total practical bypass** | | **~5.1 mb/d** | vs ~20 mb/d normal Hormuz flow |

**Petroline is not the escape hatch its nameplate suggests.** It hit 7 mb/d in March, but **Yanbu's two loading terminals handle only ~3–4 mb/d under wartime conditions**, and three domestic refineries draw ~1 mb/d before crude reaches a tanker. Effective Saudi export ceiling: **nearer 4 than 7** — and those barrels then clear Bab el-Mandeb at 62%.

**The clock: the UAE targets 3.6 mb/d of bypass by mid-2027**, new West–East line half-built and due early 2027. Saudi could add 2–4 mb/d before 2030 if FIDs are taken promptly; none announced.

**The structural bear case is 12–18 months out — and the toll regime shortens it.** A $1–2m per-vessel charge is a **permanent** tax on the Hormuz route that pipelines do not pay. **Every month the toll runs, the economics of routing around it improve, and the FID that has not been taken gets easier to justify.** Iran's revenue lever and the bypass clock work against each other, and the bypass clock does not stop when the war does.
