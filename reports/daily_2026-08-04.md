## 3. The Restraint Currently Holding — and what breaks it

**Energy infrastructure is still being deliberately spared.** The July 29 US wave hit coastal surveillance, coastal defence and maritime capability at Bandar Abbas and Kish — targets *on* the strait — and did **not** hit Kharg Island, refineries, or export terminals. The US is reported to be restraining Israel from striking Iranian energy assets. Every escalation to date has respected that line.

**What breaks it:** a mass-casualty event on a US base (the Jordan missile strike came close), or an Iranian strike that sinks rather than damages a tanker. **The Damietta FSRU hit on July 29 is the closest thing to a probe of the line so far** — it was energy infrastructure, it was on Egyptian soil, and it drew no energy-infrastructure retaliation. That non-response is itself evidence the restraint is real.

**The second limiter is now commercial, and it cuts the other way.** Even if the military line holds perfectly, war-risk underwriting is keeping barrels off the water. **De-escalation no longer automatically reopens the strait.** That is new, and it is the single most important structural change in today's report.

## 4. The Economic & Sanctions Front

**The August 21 cliff is the biggest dated event on the calendar and it was missing from this platform until this morning.** I logged it today as a new event; it should have been there in June.

| | Detail |
|---|---|
| **What expires** | Sweeping US sanctions relief permitting **dollar-denominated trade in Iranian crude for the first time in 40+ years** |
| **Date** | **August 21, 2026** — 17 days out |
| **Iranian output** | 2.9M bpd (Jun) → **4.0M bpd (Jul)** → **4.2M bpd expected (Aug)** |
| **Iranian exports** | pre-war 1.7M bpd → **~2.0M bpd** |
| **If extended** | 4.4–4.5M bpd |
| **Who buys** | **China, effectively alone** |

**Why China alone, and why that matters.** Non-Chinese refiners have already filled August-arrival requirements, and a 60-day window is too short for their internal compliance reviews to clear Iranian barrels. So the relief did not broaden the buyer base — it deepened one channel. **A single destination with no competition sets the discount.** Historical scale: at peak discount levels in 2025, competition among sanctioned exporters saved Chinese buyers up to **$28.8M per day**.

**Is enforcement credible? Partially, and the mix is the point.** The State Department took further action against illicit traders and shadow-fleet operators as recently as **July 29 — inside the relief window.** Washington is running relief and enforcement simultaneously. That tells you the relief is **transactional and reversible**, not a policy turn.

**The trade read, stated as a two-sided event because it is one:** bearish into August 21 as Iran front-loads every cargo it can; **sharply bullish after** if the waivers lapse and ~2M bpd is forced back into the shadow trade. **This is the first hard date on the board where the bull case and the bear case have the same catalyst.**

## Freight & Insurance — why the two measures disagree

| | Pre-war | Weeks ago | **Now** |

**That last row is the whole story in one number.** Avoiding the strait costs roughly **half** what transiting it costs. The market has priced a Hormuz transit at a ~$240,000/day premium over the identical voyage that starts outside it. A single sensitive VLCC voyage can exceed **$10M in war-risk cover alone.**

**Now the contradiction, which I am not going to smooth over.** Our four measures of Hormuz capacity do not agree, and they have not agreed for two weeks:

| Measure | Reading | Implied capacity | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hull count (MarineTraffic) | 14 transits/24h vs ~120 pre-war | **~12%** | Jul 30 |
| Commonwealth Bank of Australia | third-party estimate | **30–35%** | Jul 31 |
| DOE volume | 8.5M bbl/day vs ~20M normal | **~42%** | Jul 19 (**16d old**) |
| Lloyd's List, VLCC volumes | **−36%**, but voyages longer | **~64%** | undated |

**Our blend sits at 33% and no single measure confirms it.** The insurance data explains *why* the spread is so wide: **the ships still sailing are bigger, fuller, and paying enormous premiums to make one crossing worth it.** Fewer hulls, more barrels per hull, longer voyages. **Hull counts understate throughput; volume estimates overstate normality.** The truth is between, and it is closer to the middle than either tail.

**Same problem, worse, at Bab el-Mandeb — and I corrected our number for it today.** Tankers laden with Saudi crude are leaving the strait **with transponders off**, at least two confirmed dark. AIS is the *only* measure we have there. **A blockade you can measure only by AIS, being evaded specifically by turning AIS off, cannot be measured by AIS.** I raised capacity 70% → 78% and confidence 0.45 → 0.55. That is a better-reasoned estimate, not a measurement, and it is why the event's contribution fell 0.27 → 0.16.

**This is the clock, and it has two hands moving at different speeds.**

**The far hand:** the UAE line is **half built and targeting early 2027**. When it lands it takes Gulf bypass from ~8.8M bpd to ~10.6M bpd.

**Framed as the bull thesis's expiry date: the structural bear case is real and it arrives in early 2027, not this quarter.** Anyone holding a Hormuz-premium long past Q4 is betting against concrete that is already poured.
