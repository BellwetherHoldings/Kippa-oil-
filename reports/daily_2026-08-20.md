## 4. The Economic & Sanctions Front — T-1

**OFAC General License X expires 12:01 AM EDT Friday, August 21 — roughly twenty-four hours.** No renewal reported.

GL X authorised all transactions "ordinarily incident and necessary to the production, sale, delivery, or offloading" of Iranian-origin crude, petrochemicals and petroleum products. It was issued **June 22** following the **June 17** fourteen-point MOU and ran 60 days. Counsel commentary is consistent across firms (Greenberg Traurig, WilmerHale, Baker McKenzie, Holland & Knight): **there is no statutory or regulatory requirement that GL X be renewed.** It is presidential discretion. OFAC may revoke, amend, extend, or simply let it lapse.

**The base rate that matters: the MOU it was bolted to already expired on Aug 17 with no extension.** The political scaffolding under GL X is gone; the licence is standing on its own.

**What lapse actually does to barrels — less than the headline.**

| | Figure |
|---|---|
| Iranian crude + condensate exports | **1.65–1.80 mb/d** |
| Destination | almost entirely **Chinese independent refiners** |
| Mechanism | **350+ vessel shadow fleet**, STS off Malaysia, Singapore, **and increasingly the Sea of Oman** |
| Discount to Brent, 2023 | ~$8/bbl |
| **Discount to Brent, 2026** | **$14–17/bbl** |

**That trade was already built for a no-licence world and ran for years without GL X.** Lapse raises the **discount** and the **friction**; it does not remove the **volume**. Discounts already widened from ~$8 to $14–17 as Washington reactivated secondary sanctions on teapots and Hong Kong front companies.

**Enforcement credibility: real but bounded.** OFAC's October 2025 designation of 47 vessels, 18 entities and 6 individuals in the Sepehr Energy network was the most ambitious push since 2019, and at least 10 tankers have been seized since December 2025 under Operation Southern Spear. The assessment in the source literature is **"deterrent signal, limited impact."**

**And note where the UAE embargo does *not* bite.** Iranian crude does not reach China through Jebel Ali. **An import embargo is not an export interdiction.** Anyone treating yesterday's UAE announcement as a supply event is confusing the two.

**Petroline hit its physical maximum of 7 mb/d on March 11 and there is no additional bypass capacity between the eastern fields and the Red Sea.** The stated expansion would take years, cost billions, and require changes to Saudi crude's pricing mechanism. **Yanbu is the binding constraint, not the pipe:** two loading terminals handling ~3–4 mb/d under wartime conditions, with three domestic refineries drawing ~1 mb/d before crude reaches a tanker. Effective Saudi export ceiling is **nearer 4 than 7** — and those barrels then clear Bab el-Mandeb at 62%.

**The clock:** the UAE targets **3.6 mb/d of bypass by mid-2027** via a parallel ADCOP line, with the new West–East project **50% complete and targeting an early-2027 start** after the Crown Prince directed ADNOC to fast-track it. Saudi Arabia is in talks with **Kuwait, Bahrain and Qatar** — none of which has any land-based bypass out of the Gulf — about carrying their barrels too.

**The structural bear case is 12–18 months out, and the toll shortens it.** A $1–2m per-vessel charge is a **permanent** tax on the Hormuz route that pipelines do not pay. **Every month the toll runs, the FID that has not been taken gets easier to justify.** Iran's revenue lever and the bypass clock work against each other, and **the bypass clock does not stop when the war does.**
