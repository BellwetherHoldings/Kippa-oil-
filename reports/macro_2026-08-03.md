### The news layer — and the Fed is the story

- **September hike odds are now ~63%**, up from ~38% before last Wednesday's meeting. The Fed held at **3.50–3.75%** on July 29 with **three dissents in favour of a hike**, and the median 2026 dot implies one more increase. Chair Warsh has deliberately stripped forward guidance.
- **The labour market is the counterweight.** June nonfarm payrolls came in at **57,000 against a 110,000 forecast** — a bad miss — with unemployment ticking down to **4.2%**.
- **Inflation is cooling.** June headline CPI **3.5% y/y**, undershooting forecasts, down from May's **4.2%** which was itself driven by a **23.5% energy surge**. Producer prices eased too.
- **DXY around 101**, holding above its 200-day average after recovering from a four-year low below 97 earlier this year.

**The synthesis, and it matters more than the −0.05 suggests.** The Fed is being pulled two ways: hiring has stalled (57k) but inflation expectations are being driven by *oil*, and oil is the one thing the Fed cannot control. **A September hike at 63% odds is dollar-positive and oil-negative.** But note the reflexivity that has run all month — oil at $92 pushed hike odds up, the hike threat strengthened the dollar, the dollar capped oil, and oil at $79 now argues *against* a hike. **The loop is self-limiting in both directions, which is a large part of why WTI keeps mean-reverting into the low-$80s.**
