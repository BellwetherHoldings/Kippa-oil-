## 3. The Escalation Limiter — asymmetric restraint, holding

**Corrected twice this week and the second correction stands.** There is no *mutual* taboo on energy infrastructure — Iran hit Ras Laffan and Kuwait, Israel hit Asaluyeh, the UAE hit Lavan, the Houthis hit Jazan twice. **But the United States has not struck Iranian energy infrastructure.** In March the USAF hit 90+ military sites on **Kharg Island** — up to 90% of Iran's oil exports — and left the terminal intact, choosing blockade over destruction.

**That limiter held through the weekend.** No US strike on Kharg's terminal; no Iranian strike on a Gulf oil field.

**Why it matters, restated with this week's evidence behind it:** a blockade is reversible in an afternoon; a destroyed terminal is not. **The bull case therefore rests on transit friction rather than destroyed capacity** — and this week produced three independent confirmations that transit friction is easing or was overstated: ~9 mb/d transiting (Aug 13), Gulf production recovering (below), and now specs shorting the escalation.

**What breaks it:** a US strike on Kharg's terminal, or an Iranian strike on a Gulf oil **field** rather than a refinery or a hull.

## 4. The Economic / Sanctions Front

**Iranian crude at $12–$15 discount**, Chinese teapots taking **~1.5–1.8 mb/d** outside USD channels. **Russian Urals to China ~$7 under**, near the widest since 2022.

**Iran's negotiating position is now explicitly sequenced, and the sequence is the obstacle.** The foreign ministry: *"as long as the US naval blockade continues, the necessary conditions for the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz do not exist."* **Iran wants the blockade lifted first; Washington treats the blockade as the leverage that produces a deal.** That is not a gap that splits — it is a disagreement about who moves first, which historically resolves only when one side's cost curve breaks.

**And this week's data suggests neither side's cost curve is breaking.** Gulf production is recovering (§5), transit volumes are far higher than headlines imply, and oil is at $88 rather than $150. **The pressure that would force a resolution is not building — it is easing.** That is bearish for the reopening trade in both directions: less urgency to reopen, less premium if it does.

**New to this report, and it changes the shape of the bull case.** The IEA's August report puts **Gulf oil production at 23.9 mb/d in July — up 2.5 mb/d on the month, after a 3.7 mb/d rise in June**, and still 8.3 mb/d below pre-war.

**I have cited the 8.3 mb/d shortfall for three days without the trajectory behind it.** The direction matters: **Gulf production has recovered 6.2 mb/d across two months and is climbing, not deteriorating.** That is consistent with Thursday's capacity revision and with Brent near $88.

**The caveat I am not dropping:** the IEA simultaneously **widened its Q3 deficit estimate to 1.8 mb/d — more than double the prior month** — and warns inventory buffers are depleting rapidly. **Recovering production and a widening deficit are not contradictory** (demand and stock draw can outrun a supply rebound) **but they pull in opposite directions, and I am not going to present only the half that suits a view.**
