## 3. The Economic Front — where the escalation actually moved

The vector shifted from missiles to money overnight.

- **Trump wants Iran tariffs added to the bipartisan Russia/Iran sanctions bill** (reported Jul 30). Direct effect ≈ zero: the US imports almost nothing from Iran. This is signalling.
- **The one that matters is the separate 25% secondary tariff on countries trading with Iran**, announced January 12 and *still without published implementation guidance* seven months later. That gap is itself the story.
- **Iran exports ~1.3M bpd of crude — roughly 90% of it to China**, plus refined product to the UAE, Turkey, Iraq, India and Pakistan.

**Why enforcement is unlikely to bite, in the platform's read:**
- Chinese teapot refiners buy Iranian barrels at **discounts often exceeding $10/bbl to Brent**, settled in renminbi through channels insulated from the dollar system. The economics survive a tariff.
- **China issued an injunction in May blocking US sanctions on five of its refiners** — Hengli Petrochemical (Dalian) plus Shandong Jincheng, Hebei Xinhai, Shouguang Luqing and Shandong Shengxing. Beijing has already demonstrated it will simply refuse.
- The UAE, the largest buyer of Iranian fuel oil, has deep US ties and won't test it.

**Net:** strictly enforced, secondary tariffs remove >1M bpd — a larger supply event than anything the strait has produced in two weeks. On the evidence, strict enforcement is not the base case. **Watch for published implementation guidance; that is the signal, not the announcement.**

## 4. OPEC+ — the counterweight nobody is pricing

**OPEC+ approved a 188,000 bpd quota increase for August** — the fifth consecutive monthly hike unwinding the 1.65M bpd of 2023 restraint. The group is roughly **halfway through** that reversal.

Breakdown: Saudi +62k · Russia +62k · Iraq +26k · Kuwait +16k · Kazakhstan +10k · Algeria +6k · Oman +5k.

**The critical caveat, and it cuts bullish:** roughly half the restoration has happened *on paper* while actual barrels reaching refineries lag far behind. **The quota-versus-delivery gap is the defining feature of the current supply environment.** OPEC+ is announcing supply it is not fully delivering — which means the headline increases are less bearish than they look, and the group's usable spare capacity is thinner than the quota sheet implies.

## 5. Second-Order Politics Worth Tracking

- **Kazakhstan / CPC** — Black Sea terminal restarted July 27 after a week-long drone shutdown. Kazakh output had fallen to ~1.0M bpd; H1 production was 45.7Mt, **−8.4% y/y**, with the full-year target cut to 98Mt. Non-Hormuz supply, back but fragile.
- **Russia** — a joint Russia/Iran sanctions bill moving through Congress ties the two files together. Discounted Russian barrels into China have historically **crowded out Iranian crude**, which mutes the effect of squeezing Iran alone.
- **US interceptor depletion** — Patriot stocks at **759–827 vs 2,330 pre-war (−65%)**, THAAD 234–278 vs 452, with forces reportedly letting some missiles through when American lives aren't at stake. CSIS estimates three years to replenish. Not a supply factor today; it fattens the tail on every future salvo.
