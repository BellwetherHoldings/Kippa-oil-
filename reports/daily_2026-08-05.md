## 3. The Restraint Holding — and the limiter I can now name

**Energy infrastructure is still deliberately spared, and the record now shows the line was approached twice and not crossed either time.** In June, Trump announced plans to strike "VERY HARD" including **Kharg Island** and other oil infrastructure, then cancelled when final points of an agreement were approved. On August 1 he said the US was "locked and loaded and ready" and cancelled again. **The July 29 wave hit coastal surveillance and maritime capability at Bandar Abbas and Kish — targets on the strait — and did not touch export terminals.**

**And I can now name the escalation limiter with a number.** The US has expended **nearly 80% of its interceptors for key missile-defence systems**, with air-defence stockpiles particularly depleted by this war. That is a hard physical constraint on sustaining another campaign against a state with Iran's missile inventory. **It is very likely part of why CENTCOM recommended ending the bombing, and it means the "renewed strikes" Trump is threatening are a more expensive card than the rhetoric implies.**

**What breaks the restraint:** a mass-casualty event on a US base, or the talks collapsing in a way that makes Kharg Island politically necessary. Trump has explicitly framed this as Tehran's last chance.

## 4. The Economic & Sanctions Front

**The Iranian barrel: August 21 is 16 days out and unchanged.** US sanctions relief permitting dollar-denominated trade in Iranian crude expires then. Iran is front-loading — output **2.9M bpd (Jun) → 4.0M (Jul) → 4.2M expected (Aug)**, exports from a pre-war 1.7M toward **~2.0M bpd** — with **China effectively the only buyer**, since non-Chinese refiners have filled August-arrival requirements and the window is too short for their compliance reviews. Enforcement ran *inside* the window: the State Department acted against shadow-fleet operators on July 29. **The relief is transactional and reversible.**

**The Russian barrel, and this is a reversal worth flagging.** Urals discounts to India have **narrowed to $1–2/bbl against dated Brent**, down from **more than $10/bbl in early July.** The cause is this crisis: Indian refiners bid up Russian grades precisely because Hormuz disruption removed the Middle Eastern alternative. **Read the implication carefully — a Hormuz reopening does not just add Gulf barrels, it re-widens the Russian discount and pushes Urals volume back into competition. The bear case compounds.**

**Tariff pressure, and its limits.** Washington imposed a **50% tariff on Indian imports effective August 27**. India has kept buying Russian crude. **That is the enforcement-credibility answer in one line: the tariff is real, the deterrent is not.**
