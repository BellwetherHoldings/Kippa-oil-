## 3. The Escalation Limiter — asymmetric restraint, and what breaks it

**Unchanged from Aug 14, and I am not re-litigating it a fourth time.** There is no *mutual* taboo on energy infrastructure: Iran hit Ras Laffan and Kuwait, Israel hit Asaluyeh, the UAE hit Lavan, the Houthis hit Jazan twice, Ukraine is burning Russian refineries weekly. **The limiter is unilateral and American.** In March the USAF struck 90+ military sites on Kharg Island — the terminal handling up to 90% of Iran's oil exports — and deliberately left the loading infrastructure intact, choosing blockade over destruction.

**What breaks it:** a US strike on an Iranian refinery, export terminal, or the Kharg loading facilities. Reporting from Aug 1 indicated the US and Israel had prepared exactly such a campaign — power plants and refineries, up to two weeks, aiming to cut electricity to Tehran — with Trump withholding final authorisation. **That authorisation is the single largest repricing event on the board, and it has now sat un-given for fifteen days.** Escalation carries a **47.8% chance of $100+** (§10).

**The countervailing fact I have to keep on the record:** Iran has prepared a retaliation plan explicitly targeting *regional* energy infrastructure. The limiter is not restraint for its own sake — it is a recognition that removing it is symmetric.

## 4. The Economic & Sanctions Front

**The August 21 cliff is the dominant near-term item and it is five days out.** The OFAC general license — issued June 22 as the 60-day instrument attached to the June 17 US–Iran MOU — authorises production, sale, delivery and offloading of Iranian-origin crude, petrochemicals and refined products **in US dollars**, and lapses on that clock unless affirmatively renewed.

**Who actually buys the barrels: China, and essentially only China.** Non-Chinese refiners largely filled August-arrival requirements and the waiver window was too tight for their compliance reviews. Discounts on Iranian crude widened from roughly $0.50–1.00 under Brent in early 2025 to **$8–10 under Brent** by end-2025, with China taking **over 80% of Iran's shipped oil.**

**Russia and Iran are now competing for the same shrinking buyer pool, and it is a price war.** India has retreated from Russian purchases under US tariff pressure, and Urals is trading at close to its widest discounts since 2022. Both sanctioned exporters are cutting to the same Chinese refiners. **This is bearish, and it is bearish in a way the geopolitical score does not capture:** sanctioned barrels are not leaving the market, they are being *repriced into it.*

**Is enforcement credible?** Mixed, and I will not pretend otherwise. Against it: Washington eased restrictions on Russian and Iranian crude specifically to avoid a supply shock for Asian buyers — an explicit admission that full enforcement is too expensive. Senate legislation authorising up to 100% tariffs on large buyers of Russian energy remains **unenacted**. For it: State Department action against shadow-fleet operators and illicit traders continued through July 29 even while the waiver was live. **Read: enforcement is transactional and targeted, not a policy turn — which is precisely why a lapse on August 21 would matter. The relief was always a deadline, never a direction.**
