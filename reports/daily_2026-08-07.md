## 3. The Restraint Holding — intact, but the pause that protected it is gone

**Energy infrastructure is still deliberately spared.** Iran's strike today hit "hostile targets" in the strait, not export terminals. The line has now been approached three times and not crossed: June (Kharg Island, cancelled), August 1 (cancelled for negotiations), July 29 (coastal and maritime targets at Bandar Abbas and Kish, no terminals).

**But what made the restraint look durable this week was the seven-day silence, and that is over.** The limiter that remains is physical: the US has expended **~80% of its interceptors** for key missile-defence systems. That constrains the scale of any American response more than it constrains Iran.

**What breaks it now:** the rejected terms hardening into a standoff, plus one more tanker sinking. **The Bab el-Mandeb sinking on August 5 shows the threshold for that outcome has already been crossed once this week at the other chokepoint.**

## 4. The Economic & Sanctions Front

**The transit fee is no longer a rumour — it is a published demand, and it reframes the whole dispute.** Iran wants **up to 7% of cargo value** per transit and **20% of cargo** as a violation penalty, plus **compensation from "hostile" states** before passage. **That is not a navigation agreement; it is a tariff regime imposed on a waterway Iran does not own.** The US rejection was immediate.

**Put it beside the insurance number and the arithmetic is brutal.** War-risk already costs **7.5–10% of hull value**. Add a 7% cargo levy and the all-in cost of a Hormuz transit roughly doubles. **A "reopening" on these terms would not restore normal economics — it would formalise the premium.**

**The Iranian barrel — August 21 is 14 days out.** Sanctions relief permitting dollar-denominated Iranian crude trade expires then. Iran is front-loading: **2.9M bpd (Jun) → 4.0M (Jul) → 4.2M expected (Aug)**, exports toward **~2.0M bpd**, with **China effectively the only buyer**. Enforcement continued inside the window. **Relief is transactional and reversible — and today's rejection makes an extension materially less likely.**

**The Russian barrel:** Urals to India at **$1–2/bbl** under dated Brent, from **$10+/bbl in early July**. The **50% US tariff on Indian imports takes effect August 27** and India has kept buying. The tariff is real; the deterrent is not.
