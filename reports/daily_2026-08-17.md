## 4. The Economic & Sanctions Front — T-4 and wider than "barrels"

**The OFAC general license expires 12:01 AM EDT Friday, August 21. No renewal decision has been reported.**

**What lapses is bigger than the oil, and this is the part the market under-weights.** The license authorises:
1. **Unlimited** sales of Iranian crude, refined products and petrochemicals
2. Payment **in US dollars** — for the oil *and* for freight *and* for insurance
3. **Use of previously sanctioned vessels** to carry the cargo
4. Receipt of proceeds **directly into Iran's central bank**, cutting transaction costs

**A lapse does not merely re-prohibit the barrels. It re-strands the tanker fleet carrying them and forces the entire payment chain back into the discounted shadow trade.** Points 2–4 are structural plumbing, and plumbing takes longer to rebuild than a trade flow.

**Who buys: China, and the concentration is higher than I previously logged.** China now takes **roughly 90%** of Iran's oil exports — not the 80%+ I recorded from 2025-vintage data — with teapot refiners the bulk of it. Dollar clearing was expected to make Chinese buying **accelerate into the deadline**, which is the mechanism behind the bearish near-term leg.

**Discounts and the price war:** Iranian crude moved from roughly $0.50–1.00 under Brent in early 2025 to **$8–10 under** by year-end. Russia and Iran are now cutting against each other for the same shrinking Chinese buyer pool as India retreats under US tariff pressure, with Urals near its widest discount since 2022. **Sanctioned barrels are not leaving the market — they are being repriced into it.** That is a bearish force the geopolitical score does not capture.

**Is enforcement credible? Mixed, and the honest answer is "targeted, not comprehensive."** Against: Washington eased restrictions specifically to avoid a supply shock for Asian buyers — an admission that full enforcement is too expensive. Senate legislation authorising up to 100% tariffs on large buyers of Russian energy remains **unenacted**.
