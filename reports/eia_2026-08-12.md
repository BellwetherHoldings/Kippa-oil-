### Is this real, or a data artifact?

**I checked, because a 3.5-sigma print deserves it.** Weekly changes across 867 observations have a standard deviation of 5,014 kbbl. This build ranks **4th largest ever recorded**:

| Rank | Week | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2021-02-26 | +21,563 |
| 2 | 2020-04-10 | +19,248 |
| 3 | 2023-01-06 | +18,961 |
| **4** | **2026-08-07** | **+17,423** |
| 5 | 2023-02-10 | +16,283 |

The comparables are the Texas freeze aftermath, the COVID demand collapse, and a January 2023 refinery-outage week. **Distinguished company, but not unprecedented.**

**The mechanism is visible in the same release, which is the strongest argument that it is real:** US crude imports jumped to **7,339 kb/d — the 97th percentile of three years, a +1,650 kb/d four-week change** — while refinery utilization slipped 96.5% → 96.2%. **A record import surge into flat runs produces exactly this build.** The number is internally consistent, not an isolated outlier.

**One caveat kept on the record:** EIA noted a re-benchmarking adjustment to the production estimate in this release, described as under 50,000 b/d (~0.18%). That is far too small to explain a 17.4 mb build and does not account for it — noted so it is not later invoked as a hidden explanation.

**The interesting question this raises, which I cannot answer yet:** imports hit the 97th percentile while Hormuz runs at 15% capacity. US imports are largely Canadian, Mexican and Latin American, so those are not the same barrels — but a surge of this size suggests either buyers front-running further disruption or cargoes being diverted to the US because they cannot be placed elsewhere. **Either reading is bearish for crude in the near term and neither is bullish.**
